Published by UNITED UAV Official

A folded UA20 can be smaller than the space reserved for it in a van and still be too tall to pass under the rear door. The buyer needs to check four places where the load can interfere with its surroundings. These are the entrance, the path between the entrance and the bay, the bay itself, and the area where the aircraft is prepared after unloading. The dimensions in the [UA20 product specifications](https://store.uniteduav.com/products/ua20-20l-agricultural-drone?utm_source=mintlify&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=mintlify_ua20_vehicle_loading_clearance_20261108&utm_content=ua20_vehicle_loading_clearance) give the first set of inputs. Use the method below to decide which measurements to request, which measurements to take on the actual vehicle, and what evidence to retain before accepting the layout.

## Assign each published dimension to a physical state

The seller's specifications give three overall dimension triples, each associated with a different arrangement of the arms and propellers. Keep the original order of the dimensions when transferring them into a worksheet. On your loading drawing, label which horizontal axis runs along the vehicle and which runs across it. Ask for a dimensioned outline that shows the axes and confirms the equipment fitted when the supplier measured the aircraft. A reversed approach to the door, a different storage orientation, and a diagonal approach are separate cases on that drawing.

| Seller-listed aircraft state | Overall dimensions |
| --- | --- |
| Arms and propellers folded | 870 × 530 × 680 mm |
| Arms open, propellers folded | 1405 × 1270 × 680 mm |
| Arms and propellers open | 1862 × 1727 × 680 mm |

The horizontal footprint changes substantially while the listed height stays at 680 mm. That common height is useful for a first pass. The loaded height at the door will also depend on whatever supports or encloses the aircraft. A baseboard, protective case, trolley or loading platform changes the vertical reference. A handle or latch can control the width at one elevation, while the widest part of the aircraft controls it at another. Draw separate outlines for the quoted aircraft, its proposed enclosure, and the combination that actually moves through the doorway.

Use a state name on every measurement sheet. For example, a supplier may measure the aircraft with the arms and propellers folded, while the buyer may be checking the dimensions of a closed transport case. Include the case identifier and the installed equipment in the record for the second measurement. With this distinction, a buyer can compare the numbers directly and see what remains unmeasured. If equipment is changed before acceptance, the team can locate the affected drawing and check it against the delivered configuration.

## Measure the route from outside the vehicle to the final bay

Start at the point where the case or loading aid first approaches the vehicle. Photograph the door held in its normal loading position. Measure between the actual obstructions and identify the height of each one above the surface that carries the load. Hinges, seals, check straps and a latch can all reduce the usable opening inside the nominal frame. A measurement at floor level can overstate usable width if the latch sits at the same height as the case shoulder. A simple sketch with the pinch point, its height and its location along the route is more useful than the nominal door size from a vehicle brochure.

Take width readings at the elevations that correspond to the lowest, widest and highest parts of the load. For a stepped case, place each reading alongside the relevant section of the outline. Record the usable vertical distance from the load support surface to the header. If a trolley deck, sill or platform changes the height while the load enters, use the actual height of its deck at that point. A ramp can change the angle of the load as its leading corner passes beneath the header. Inspect the corner path during the trial and record the tightest location. A height measured from the van floor describes only the part of the movement where the load rests on that floor.

Continue along the same route inside the vehicle. Mark the first place the load must turn, the space available at that turn and the point where its direction straightens again. Map wheel housings, shelving, partitions and fixed cabinets against the load at their actual heights. An obstruction that matters to the trolley wheels may leave the case body free. A high cabinet corner may do the opposite. Measure the final bay with the door closed and with the rest of the load in its intended positions. A bay dimension on its own tells the buyer little about whether the route to that bay is available.

Repeat the measurement for unloading if the order of movements or the vehicle orientation will differ. A case may be able to slide in straight when an adjacent compartment is empty, then require a turn on the way out once the rest of the equipment is in place. Record the intended loading sequence and mark each accessory that must be moved to regain access. The result should describe a repeatable layout for the assigned vehicle and equipment, including any space that has to be reserved outside the door.

## Work the opening and the turn as separate calculations

Consider a hypothetical rear opening with a minimum effective width of 660 mm across the relevant part of the case and a vertical clearance of 780 mm above a level loading surface. Assume the buyer's closed case measures 940 × 610 × 760 mm, with the 940 mm axis parallel to the direction of entry. Suppose the buyer allocates 20 mm on each side and 25 mm above the case. These allowances belong to this example and can be replaced by project requirements.

On a straight approach the required width is 610 plus 40, or 650 mm. The width passes this screen by 10 mm. The required height is 760 plus 25, or 785 mm. The opening falls short of the example height requirement by 5 mm. Measured without the buyer's allowance there would be 20 mm of headroom. That smaller gap explains how the case could physically clear the frame in one trial yet fail the specified acceptance check. Testing the same opening with the listed folded aircraft height of 680 mm would answer a different question.

Now suppose a fixed partition requires the entire rectangular case to yaw through 20 degrees inside a straight section of corridor that is 880 mm wide. As viewed from above, the case projects part of its length across the corridor. For an angle between zero and ninety degrees measured from the corridor axis, the projected width is the case width times the cosine of the angle plus the case length times the sine of the angle. Here, 610 × cos(20°) plus 940 × sin(20°) is approximately 895 mm. In this stated corridor, the turning case already exceeds the width by about 15 mm before any side clearance is added.

This geometric check assumes that the whole case occupies the constant-width section at the stated angle. At a thin door opening, different corners can cross the jamb at different times. There, trace the corners along the proposed motion or use a full-size surrogate to test the actual opening. A projection is useful for rejecting a turn when the model applies. Use a path trace to locate the controlling obstruction and distinguish a fixed clearance problem from a change in approach angle.

## Check the destination bay in its occupied condition

For the same example, suppose the reserved bay measures 1100 × 720 × 840 mm with clear space over its entire rectangular footprint. Subtracting the 940 × 610 × 760 mm case leaves 160 mm in length, 110 mm in width and 80 mm in height. Those are total residual dimensions. The actual position of the case determines how much is on each side. Mark its intended position on the floor and record the distances to both adjacent surfaces. If the case must be close to one wall to accommodate another item, the whole 110 mm cannot be counted as usable clearance on that wall.

Identify anything that can occupy the residual space during loading, transport or unloading. Door hardware can travel inward as the door closes. A handle can require hand space beyond the rigid outline. A nearby cabinet may have to open before the case can be accessed. Measure these interferences as movements on the layout. Include the access space needed to inspect the case and the supplier-specified restraint arrangement. A vehicle or restraint specialist can assess the load and attachment points using the delivered configuration and the vehicle documentation.

The published default package presentation lists the complete aircraft, one G12 controller, two standard 14S 20Ah battery packs and one U6Q charger. The store also presents extra-battery choices and a 30 L solid spreader option. Turn the selected items into a packing list with a measured outline and assigned position for each container. Confirm the actual quantities and packing arrangement in the written offer. A controller case or spreader container can occupy the turning pocket even when the aircraft's parking space stays the same. Ask the supplier to identify whether the optional equipment is fitted or packed separately when providing dimensions for your configuration.

## Reserve room for the transition between aircraft states

The seller lists the middle state with the arms open and propellers folded at 1405 × 1270 mm in plan. Relative to the listed folded rectangle, those overall axes increase by 535 mm and 740 mm. Opening the propellers takes the listed horizontal dimensions to 1862 × 1727 mm, an increase of 457 mm along each overall axis relative to the middle state. These are differences between overall rectangles. The location of each arm hinge, propeller tip and other protrusion determines where that space is needed.

Mark a reference point for the aircraft on the work-area plan and show its intended orientation. Overlay the three end states using the dimensioned outline when it is available. Then ask the supplier to identify the outward path of the farthest points during the approved handling sequence. Record the boundary traced in that movement as a separate line. The three end states alone leave the position of those intermediate points undetermined. A wall, vehicle door or post can be clear of both end positions and still interfere with a moving part between them.

The worksheet should include any part of the staging area that must stay available while the aircraft changes state. Identify the space occupied by a person, the support surface and the equipment being fitted or removed. On the site plan, assign a place for the closed case and loose equipment after unloading. This keeps the preparation space free for the movement the buyer is trying to verify. When space is tight, have the supplier demonstrate the stated configuration change with the agreed clearances and record which part comes closest to a fixed obstacle. Use the demonstration to check the geometry of the layout.

## Use a full-size check to find the smallest clearance

A rigid, lightweight surrogate of the closed case gives the buyer a way to check corners and dimensions before the equipment is delivered. Include protruding handles and feet. If a loading aid determines the height or orientation, model its outline and the position it holds the case in. Compare the surrogate with the supplier's drawing in all three axes and record where its shape differs. A smaller or flexible surrogate can pass through a route that would reject the actual case. Mark that test as a screening result and repeat it with the supplied configuration when it arrives.

At each pinch point, measure the remaining gap in the direction that would close first. A slot beside a handle may need a horizontal measurement, while a header over an inclined ramp may need a check at the highest corner during the motion. Ask a second observer to locate the minimum independently if the result is close to the agreed allowance. Record the smallest observed gap and the measurement uncertainty where it affects the decision. If the difference between the measured gap and the required allowance is smaller than the uncertainty, seek a more reliable measurement or provide additional clearance before signing off.

For final acceptance, repeat the check with the specified case, the intended loading aid and the other containers in their assigned positions. Include the unloading path and the movement into the designated preparation area. Run the check for the exact vehicle or bay layout that will be used. The door opening angle, the height of the loaded platform and the position of adjacent equipment can differ between otherwise similar vehicles. Keep a short annotated sequence of photographs or a drawing that shows where each decisive measurement was taken. A picture of the aircraft already parked in the bay documents only the last position.

## Record a fit decision that can be repeated after delivery

Give each location a line in the acceptance record. A useful line names the vehicle, the door or obstacle, the aircraft or case state, the orientation, the minimum measured gap and the buyer's required allowance. Include the revision of the case drawing and the date of measurement. For a turn, note the case angle and where the minimum was recorded. For staging, describe the movement being observed and the nearest obstacle. Assign a result of pass, fail or pending evidence to each line and keep any corrective action with the measurement it affects.

| Record item | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- |
| Aircraft and case | Quoted configuration, drawing revision, measured outline and loading aid |
| Entrance and turn | Effective openings at load height, corner path, smallest gaps and approach orientation |
| Parking and access | Occupied bay sketch, case position, closed-door check and allocated accessory spaces |
| Preparation area | Reference position, applicable aircraft states, observed moving boundary and nearest obstruction |
| Acceptance | Buyer allowances, results for loading and unloading, exceptions, owner and retest date |

For the hypothetical case above, the bay would be recorded as provisionally acceptable on static dimensions. The opening would fail the buyer's example height requirement and the stated corridor would fail the turn. The action belongs on those two lines. A lower loading platform could affect the height check, while moving the partition or changing the approach might affect the turning check. Each change needs a fresh measurement at the relevant obstruction. The bay result stays useful as evidence for a later review of the revised layout.

Keep the accepted drawing with the purchase record. A different case, a new trolley, a changed partition or an additional spreader container can invalidate a specific measurement. Define those changes as retest triggers so the fleet team can repeat the affected checks after delivery. When you review the [UA20 aircraft and available configurations](https://store.uniteduav.com/products/ua20-20l-agricultural-drone?utm_source=mintlify&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=mintlify_ua20_vehicle_loading_clearance_20261108&utm_content=ua20_vehicle_loading_clearance), send the supplier the measured entrance, the proposed loading direction and the available turning space. Request the packed outline and equipment list for the configuration in the quotation. Ask for the drawing reference and the condition in which the dimensions were measured so the delivered system can be checked against the same record.
