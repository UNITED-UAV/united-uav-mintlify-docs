The DJI Agras T30 plunger-pump listing contains individual seals, drive components, housings, mounting parts, fasteners, a signal cable, a motor module, and a one-way valve module. It is therefore not a single “pump replacement” option and it is not a diagnostic chart. A low-flow complaint, visible leak, abnormal sound, trapped air, or app warning may involve different parts of the spraying system, and more than one condition can produce a similar field symptom.

This guide provides a disciplined way to collect evidence, identify catalog items, and hand a repair case to a qualified technician. It does not prescribe internal pump disassembly, sealing compounds, lubricants, fastener torque, electrical tests, or airworthiness approval. Those decisions require the correct DJI service information, chemical-safety controls, calibrated tools, and an authorized maintenance process.

## Start with the hydraulic system, not a guessed component

The T30 manual describes a spraying system with plunger pumps and a continuous liquid-level gauge. It also treats the pump, flow meter, hoses, nozzles, tank, and app settings as parts of one operating system. That distinction should shape diagnosis. “The aircraft sprays less than expected” is an operational observation, not evidence that the motor module, plunger, bearing, valve, or a particular seal has failed.

Before selecting a part, record the conditions under which the symptom appears:

- aircraft model and serial or fleet identifier;
- pump position and any visible label or material number;
- liquid used, including whether it is water or a formulated agricultural mixture;
- nozzle model, commanded spray rate, flight mode, and relevant app warning;
- whether the tank had just been refilled or the system had been opened;
- whether the symptom affects all outlets or only part of the spray pattern;
- whether flow is consistently low, pulsating, delayed, intermittent, or absent;
- exact location and timing of any leak;
- presence of bubbles in clear hose sections;
- new noise, vibration, heat, odor, damaged wiring, or loose mounting;
- maintenance, impact, transport, washing, or chemical exposure immediately before the event.

This record separates an ordering decision from an unsupported diagnosis. Air in the hoses can affect sprinkler performance, and the T30 provides an automatic trapped-air discharge function. Nozzle selection and liquid viscosity can affect flow measurement and calibration. A blocked hose or nozzle can change system behavior without proving an internal pump defect. Conversely, a clean nozzle does not clear the pump, its electrical supply, signal cable, sealing chain, or one-way valve. Work through the system boundary before narrowing the case.

## Understand the pump as four interacting chains

A useful service triage divides the catalog into four interacting chains: energy and control, motion conversion, fluid control, and structural support. This model prevents a technician or buyer from treating a visible seal as the only possible cause of a leak, or treating an app warning as proof that the motor itself has failed.

### 1. Energy and control

The motor module at callout 16 supplies electromechanical drive, while the signal cable at callout 7 is a separate catalog item. A pump that does not run may involve the motor module, cable, connector, upstream supply, control command, or another system condition. Capture app messages and aircraft logs before disconnecting parts. Photograph the cable route and both connector faces. Record bent pins, moisture, contamination, abrasion, pinched insulation, loose retention, and prior splices without attempting to prove electrical continuity from appearance alone.

Do not replace the motor module merely because the pump is silent. Likewise, do not clear the motor merely because it can turn. A module can operate intermittently, be commanded incorrectly, or encounter excessive mechanical resistance. Electrical measurements, current limits, insulation requirements, and bench-test methods are outside the product listing and must come from approved service information.

### 2. Motion conversion and guidance

The thrust ball bearing (9), oblique disc (6), leading block (10), plunger part (14), and plunger spring (15) form the listing's motion-related group. Together, their names suggest conversion and guidance of motor rotation into reciprocating pump action, but the public catalog does not define their installed clearances, wear limits, orientation, preload, lubrication, or acceptable surface condition.

Abnormal sound, roughness, cyclical vibration, metal debris, or uneven delivery are relevant observations. They are not one-to-one diagnoses. A damaged mounting rubber can transmit normal pump vibration differently. Trapped air can create irregular delivery. A restriction can change load. A worn guide or bearing can affect motion. Preserve the evidence and avoid running a visibly damaged or leaking pump simply to make a recording. If rotation, internal alignment, or lubrication is in question, keep the aircraft out of service until the approved inspection method is available.

### 3. Fluid control and sealing

The one-way valve module (28) and the plunger-related components manage fluid movement, while at least seven separately identified sealing items control different interfaces: outer cover seal (1), star-shaped sealing ring (11), O-shape guiding ring (12), gasket (13), connector sealing ring (18), inner cover seal (24), water seal (25), and motor sealing ring (29). These parts should not be collapsed into a generic “seal kit” unless an actual kit and its contents are confirmed.

Leak location matters. Record whether moisture first appears at the hose connection, cover perimeter, motor interface, pump body joint, service port, or an unrelated line above the pump. Clean, dry, and observe only under an approved, safe test procedure. Liquid running down the housing can make a higher leak appear to originate at a lower seal. Staining after storage does not prove the leak is active. A technician should also record the chemical used because seal compatibility, swelling, residue, and cleaning requirements may depend on the formulation. The listing does not state elastomer material or authorize substitute seals.

### 4. Housing, mounting, and service-port control

The bottom base module (8), cover module (21), main part (26), mounting rubber (23), grease-hole plug (17), grease-hole magnet (19), and cataloged fasteners maintain alignment, enclosure, mounting, and service access. Housing cracks, distorted mating surfaces, loose mounting, and damaged threads can defeat a new seal. Replacing only the wet component without establishing why it failed can lead to a repeat leak.

Photograph the pump in its installed position before removal. Include the aircraft reference orientation, mounting-rubber condition, hose and cable routes, fastener locations, and witness marks. Keep fasteners tagged by position. The product page provides exact identifiers but no torque values, tightening sequence, thread treatment, or re-use criteria. Never derive those requirements from a fastener's English catalog name.

## Separate air, measurement, restriction, and pump evidence

Four issues frequently become mixed together in a low-flow complaint.

**Trapped air:** DJI's T30 manual explicitly states that bubbles in sprinkler hoses may affect sprinkler performance. It provides an automatic trapped-air discharge function, activated either by holding the spray button for two seconds or through the Clear Trapped Air control in Operation View. If the system was recently opened or the tank ran empty, document whether the approved discharge procedure was completed before concluding that a mechanical part has failed.

**Flow measurement:** The manual instructs users to calibrate the flow meter before first use and identifies recalibration triggers: installing a different nozzle model, using a liquid of different viscosity, or seeing more than a 15 percent difference between actual and theoretical values for completed area. Flow-meter evidence is not identical to pump evidence. A displayed flow anomaly may require verification of calibration state and system conditions before a pump component is ordered.

**Restriction:** The pre-flight checklist requires the spraying system not to be blocked. A restriction can exist at a hose, connector, filter, nozzle, or other point. Record whether the symptom is system-wide or isolated, and inspect under the applicable maintenance procedure. Do not use unapproved pressure, wire, solvents, or compressed air merely to force a blockage clear.

**Pump or drive condition:** Once trapped air, configuration, measurement, and external restrictions are addressed, the remaining evidence may support inspection of the pump, motor, cable, valve, seals, and motion components. That sequence does not prove a particular part is defective; it prevents a premature order from substituting for diagnosis.

## Build a repeatable evidence package

A good service package should let a second technician understand the case without relying on a chat history. Use the following structure.

1. **Machine identity:** T30 model, fleet identifier, pump position, hours or cycles if the organization tracks them, firmware/app context, and date.
2. **Complaint in measurable terms:** commanded spray rate, displayed flow, liquid volume before and after a controlled interval, nozzle configuration, liquid type, and whether the symptom is repeatable. Do not invent precision beyond the available instruments.
3. **Operating context:** temperature, recent refill, recent line opening, chemical change, nozzle change, washdown, impact, transport, or earlier repair.
4. **App and log evidence:** exact warning text, timestamp, screenshots, and exported records where available. Preserve the original file rather than transcribing only a conclusion.
5. **Installed overview:** full aircraft, pump location, mounting, cable routing, hose routing, connectors, and adjacent components.
6. **Close-up evidence:** material labels, wet traces, cracks, abrasion, pins, seals visible without unauthorized disassembly, fastener positions, and the numbered catalog image marked with the suspected callout.
7. **Checks completed:** trapped-air discharge, configuration review, external blockage inspection, calibration status, and the approved test used. Record results and who performed them.
8. **Open questions:** internal inspection required, seller package quantity, missing callout, repeated material number, required service data, or unresolved compatibility.

This record converts “pump weak” into a traceable service problem. It also protects purchasing from ordering the most expensive module simply because it is the most recognizable option.

## Use symptom patterns carefully

Symptom patterns are valuable for prioritizing inspection, but they must be treated as hypotheses.

| Observation | Evidence to collect first | Catalog groups that may require inspection, not automatic replacement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No pump response | app command, warning, cable/connector condition, supply/control evidence, mechanical freedom under approved procedure | signal cable 7, motor module 16, motion group 6/9/10/14/15 |
| Low but steady delivery | trapped-air status, nozzle model, viscosity, restriction, flow-meter calibration state, commanded versus measured output | valve 28, plunger 14, sealing chain, drive group |
| Pulsating or irregular delivery | bubbles, tank level, line condition, repeatability, sound/vibration, one-way flow behavior under approved test | valve 28, plunger/spring 14/15, drive guidance 6/9/10, seals |
| External leak | first wet point, static versus operating condition, chemical, housing and connector condition | seals 1/11/12/13/18/24/25/29, cover 21, main part 26 |
| New noise or vibration | mounting rubber, fasteners, contact marks, bearing/drive evidence, whether noise changes with load | mounting 23, bearing 9, drive group, housing modules |
| Flow display disagrees with output | calibration history, liquid viscosity, nozzle change, actual volume and area records | flow-meter/system configuration first; pump parts only if independent evidence supports it |

The table deliberately avoids “symptom equals part.” Agricultural spray systems are coupled systems, and evidence can point to more than one branch. A strong diagnosis explains why competing causes were ruled out using approved checks.

## Control the order before checkout

For each requested item, write a complete order line containing:

- aircraft model: DJI Agras T30;
- product-page URL and verification date;
- diagram callout;
- full material number, including every dot and suffix;
- exact catalog name;
- selected store variant and displayed SKU;
- required service quantity;
- seller-confirmed sales unit and included contents;
- installed position or interface;
- technician approval and supporting photo reference.

For example, “T30 / callout 18 / `YC.XJ.QT000350.01` / Pump Connector Sealing Ring / quantity 1 pending seller package confirmation” is auditable. “Pump O-ring” is not. The listing contains several rings and seals whose locations and material numbers differ.

The same discipline applies to assemblies. Before ordering the motor module, cover module, bottom base module, main part, or one-way valve module, ask what the selected unit includes. Do not assume a housing module includes its seals or fasteners. Do not assume the main part includes the valve, motor, or cable. If a quotation bundles items, compare the bundle against the individual approved lines and document the substitution.

For callouts 22 and 27, include the numbered option and store SKU context even though both show `YC.WJ.L00870.02`. If the supplier confirms the parts are physically identical, retain that confirmation with the order. If the options represent different positions or packages, the confirmation prevents a same-number assumption from causing a quantity or installation error.

## Receiving inspection is not return-to-service approval

When parts arrive, compare the supplier label and packing record with the approved order: T30 model, material number, callout, name, quantity, and any revision or kit contents. Photograph labels before discarding packaging. Quarantine a component if the code differs, the label is unreadable, a seal is deformed, a connector is damaged, a housing has impact marks, or the package contents do not match the quotation.

Fit and appearance alone are not compatibility evidence. A ring that sits in a groove may still have the wrong cross-section or material. A fastener that threads into a hole may still have the wrong length, strength, coating, or service requirement. A motor connector that mates may still represent a different module or revision. Resolve discrepancies before installation.

After installation by a qualified technician, acceptance should proceed from component checks to system checks and then to aircraft release. The exact internal assembly, torque, lubrication, electrical, and leak-test procedures must come from approved service data. At the system level, the T30 user manual requires secure parts and cables, a spraying system free of blockage, and hoses clear of bubbles. It also provides the automatic trapped-air discharge and flow-meter calibration procedures. Use those functions only in the proper operating context and follow the current manual displayed for the aircraft and app version.

A successful ground spray does not, by itself, prove that internal assembly, mounting, calibration, leak integrity, or flight safety is acceptable. Record static and operating observations, app status, commanded and observed flow, leak checks, calibration result when required, and the technician's release decision. If the system continues to leak, produces inconsistent output, generates abnormal noise, or reports a fault, stop and reopen the service case rather than normalizing the symptom.

## Practical answers to common parts questions

**Should a low-flow report start with a new motor module?** No. First preserve the evidence and distinguish trapped air, flow-meter calibration, nozzle or liquid changes, restrictions, electrical/control issues, and mechanical evidence. The motor module is only one branch of the system.

**Are the inner and outer cover rings interchangeable?** The listing does not establish interchangeability. Callout 1 (`YC.JG.MY000270.01`) and callout 24 (`YC.JG.MY000271.01`) have different names, positions, and material numbers.

**Can any similar O-ring replace the star-shaped seal or guiding ring?** No substitution is supported by the catalog. The star-shaped seal at callout 11 and O-shape guiding ring at callout 12 are separate components. Material, geometry, and chemical compatibility must be confirmed through approved sources.

**Does a flow-meter calibration fix a mechanical pump fault?** Calibration corrects the measurement relationship under the conditions specified in the manual; it is not a mechanical repair. A failed calibration should be investigated using the app guidance and approved service process.

**Why are there two variants with material number `YC.WJ.L00870.02`?** The store exposes them at callouts 22 and 27 with different SKU suffixes. The public listing does not explain the commercial distinction. Preserve both option identifiers and ask the seller before ordering.

**What should happen if the required part appears as callout 2?** Stop and request clarification. The numbered image includes the position, but the current product menu does not expose callout 2 as a selectable variant. Do not choose a neighboring option.

**Can the product diagram be used as a rebuild sequence?** No. It is an identification image. It does not provide disassembly order, cleanliness requirements, orientation, dimensions, wear limits, torque, lubrication, or test criteria.
